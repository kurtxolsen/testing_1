# Insurance Claims 101 (Homeowner Explanations)

Plain-language explanations you can use word-for-word at the door or table.

## How a storm claim works

"Your homeowner's policy covers sudden storm damage — hail and wind. You
pay your deductible, the insurance company pays the rest of what it costs
to put your roof back the way it was. It's not a discount program; it's
literally what you've been paying premiums for."

## ACV vs. RCV (depreciation)

- **RCV (Replacement Cost Value):** what it costs to replace the roof today.
- **ACV (Actual Cash Value):** RCV minus depreciation for age/wear.
- Most policies pay ACV up front, then release the **recoverable
  depreciation** after the work is completed and invoiced.

Homeowner version: "You get two checks — one up front, one after the work
is done. The second one is why finishing the job with a licensed contractor
matters: unfinished work forfeits it."

## The deductible

- It's set by the policy, not by us. Paying it is a legal requirement —
  anyone offering to 'waive' or 'eat' it is committing insurance fraud in
  most states and putting the HOMEOWNER's claim at risk.

## "Will my rates go up?"

"Storm claims are 'Act of God' claims — carriers can't single you out for
filing one. What actually happens after a big storm is the whole ZIP code's
rates adjust, whether you filed or not. Not filing just means you paid the
rate increase AND kept the damaged roof."

## Supplements

If the adjuster's scope misses items (code-required drip edge, ice & water
shield, steep/high charges), we submit a supplement with photos and code
citations. The homeowner doesn't pay for missed scope — the carrier does.

## Filing window

Most policies require claims within **one year** of the storm date (some
two). After that, the damage becomes a maintenance issue = homeowner pays.
This is the honest urgency behind "sooner is better."
