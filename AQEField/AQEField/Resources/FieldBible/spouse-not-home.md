# Spouse Not Home

## OBJECTION: "I need to talk to my wife/husband first"
**Tags:** spouse, wife, husband, talk it over, decision
**Category:** Spouse Not Home

### The Reframe
Agree instantly — then convert "talk it over" into "let's get them the facts to talk over," which requires the inspection.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Absolutely — this is a both-of-you decision, and I'd never ask you to make it alone. But here's the thing: right now there's nothing to talk over, because we don't know if there's damage. Let me do the inspection while I'm here, and tonight you two can look at actual photos of your actual roof instead of guessing. What evening are you both home so I can walk you through what I find?"

### The Psychology
Rolling with resistance instead of fighting it (aikido close). The inspection is framed as *input to* the spousal conversation, not a bypass of it. Ending with a both-present appointment sets up the real decision meeting and eliminates the one-legged close problem.

## OBJECTION: "My husband handles all the house stuff"
**Tags:** spouse, husband, handles it, not my department, defer
**Category:** Spouse Not Home

### The Reframe
Don't pitch the non-decider — recruit them as your inside advocate and lock a time with the decider.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Perfect — then he's exactly who I need to meet. Can you do me one favor? When he gets home, tell him a roofer found hail hits on the gutters — he'll know what that means. I'm back in this neighborhood Thursday; is 6 or 6:30 better for me to catch him?"

### The Psychology
Gives the spouse a tiny, specific, zero-commitment task (messenger role), which creates engagement without pressure. "He'll know what that means" flatters the decider before you've met. Alternative-choice close on the time slot ("6 or 6:30") skips the yes/no question entirely.

## OBJECTION: "We'll discuss it and call you back"
**Tags:** call back, discuss, think, later, brush off
**Category:** Spouse Not Home

### The Reframe
"We'll call you" is a polite no. Trade the vague callback for a concrete, low-stakes next step you control.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Sounds good — and so the conversation's worth having, let me leave you more than a business card. I'll email you the inspection photos and a one-page summary tonight, and I'll swing back Thursday evening to answer whatever questions come up. If the answer's no thanks, you can tell me at the door in ten seconds — deal?"

### The Psychology
Replaces their vague commitment (which decays to nothing) with your concrete one (photos tonight, Thursday visit). Pre-authorizing an easy "no" paradoxically lowers defenses and increases the odds of a real conversation — people resist pressure, not check-ins.

## OBJECTION: "She's not home and I can't sign anything without her"
**Tags:** spouse, not home, sign, can't commit, alone
**Category:** Spouse Not Home

### The Reframe
Perfect — nothing today requires a signature. Decouple the free inspection from the commitment and bank the progress.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Good news — there's nothing to sign today. The inspection is free and the photos belong to you either way; all I need is permission to set my ladder up. Then when you're both home I'll walk you through the findings together, and any decisions get made with both names on the paperwork."

### The Psychology
Foot-in-the-door: ladder permission is a micro-yes that builds commitment momentum without triggering the "big decision" alarm. Explicitly promising both-spouse paperwork later signals respect for the marriage's decision process, which is often what this objection is actually testing.
