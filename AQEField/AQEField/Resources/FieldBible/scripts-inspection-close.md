# Inspection & Kitchen-Table Close

## Setting the inspection

"All I need is about 15 minutes on the roof and 10 with you and
[SPOUSE if mentioned] afterward to walk through the photos. If both
decision-makers can be there, you'll only have to hear this once."

Always set a specific time. "Sometime this week" is not an appointment.

## Presenting findings (the photo walk)

1. Start with the collateral: dented gutters, downspouts, window wraps,
   AC fins. Soft metals don't lie — they corroborate hail size.
2. Then the roof photos: chalk-circled impacts, test square counts.
3. Explain the standard: "Insurance looks for [X] impacts per 10x10 test
   square. You have [Y]."
4. Shut up. Let them look. The photos close, not you.

## The claim close

"Based on what I documented, this qualifies. Here's what happens next: we
call your carrier together — takes about ten minutes — you tell them you
have storm damage and a contractor's inspection report. They assign an
adjuster, I meet the adjuster here so nothing gets missed, and your total
out-of-pocket is your deductible. Should we make that call now while I'm
here?"

**Key:** "call together, now, while I'm here." Momentum dies overnight.

## If they want to think about it

"Absolutely — what specifically do you want to think over? The damage is
documented either way; the only thing waiting changes is the filing window."
(Then handle the real objection — see Objections section.)

## Contract signing

- Walk every line. No rushed signatures — rushed signers cancel.
- Set expectations: adjuster meeting → approval → build day → final invoice
  to carrier for depreciation.
- Book the adjuster-meeting date placeholder before you leave the table.
