# Sales Psychology (Door & Table)

The principles behind every script in this Bible.

## The big five at the door

1. **Social proof** — "your neighbors at the gray house" beats any pitch.
   People do what the street does. This is why pins cluster.
2. **Loss aversion** (Kahneman) — losses hurt ~2x more than equivalent
   gains feel good. Frame inaction as the costly path: "the filing window
   closes, the deductible becomes the whole roof."
3. **Reciprocity** — the free inspection with a genuine no-damage-honesty
   guarantee creates obligation without pressure.
4. **Authority** — hail gauge on the belt, chalk in hand, carrier language
   in your mouth. Look like the inspector, not the salesman.
5. **Commitment & consistency** — small yeses stack: 15 minutes → photo
   walk → carrier call → contract. Never jump a step.

## Reactance (why pushing backfires)

People defend their freedom to say no. Every script here offers an out
("if there's nothing up there, I'll tell you"). Giving the out lowers the
guard; removing it raises it.

## Anchoring

Whoever says the first number sets the frame. Say "deductible" before
their brain says "$20,000 roof."

## The 7-second rule

Judgments form before your second sentence. Clean shirt, name + company +
reason + neighbor reference, two steps back off the porch, smile before
the door opens.

## Silence

After the photo walk and after the close question: stop talking. The first
person to speak after "Should we make that call now?" usually decides the
outcome. Let it be them.

## State management (yours)

Knocking is a numbers game with feelings attached. The rings on your
dashboard exist because behavior you measure is behavior you sustain.
100 knocks is ~25 conversations is ~5 inspections is ~2 contracts —
a "no" is just the cost of the next yes.
