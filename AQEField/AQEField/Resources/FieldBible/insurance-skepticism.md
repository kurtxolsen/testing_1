# Insurance Skepticism

## OBJECTION: "I don't want my rates to go up"
**Tags:** rates, premium, increase, insurance, afraid to file
**Category:** Insurance Skepticism

### The Reframe
Separate act-of-God claims from at-fault claims. Their fear is built on the car-insurance model, which doesn't apply here.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Totally reasonable fear — but hail claims don't work like a car wreck. Storm damage is an act of God, so carriers raise rates by ZIP code after a storm, not by who filed. Here's the kicker: your neighbors are filing right now, which means you're likely paying the area increase either way — the only question is whether you get a roof out of it."

### The Psychology
Corrects a false mental model (personal fault → premium hike) with the true one (territory-rated catastrophe losses). The closer — "paying the increase either way" — is an inequity frame: not filing means subsidizing the neighbors who did.

## OBJECTION: "I don't want to deal with my insurance company"
**Tags:** hassle, insurance, paperwork, adjuster, stress
**Category:** Insurance Skepticism

### The Reframe
The dread is administrative, not financial. Sell the concierge, not the claim.

### Word-for-Word Response
"That's exactly the part I take off your plate. You make one phone call to report the claim — I'll even sit with you while you do it — and from there I handle the documentation, meet the adjuster on your roof, and speak their language so you don't have to. Your total time investment is about twenty minutes."

### The Psychology
Effort discounting: people reject outcomes they want when the process feels heavy. Quantifying the burden ("one call, twenty minutes") collapses an imagined weeks-long ordeal into a concrete, small task. Offering to sit with them during the call removes the scariest single step.

## OBJECTION: "My insurance already denied my neighbor's claim"
**Tags:** denied, neighbor, claim, carrier, no point
**Category:** Insurance Skepticism

### The Reframe
A denial next door is a data point about documentation quality, not about the storm. Differentiate on evidence.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Do you know who documented your neighbor's roof? That's usually the whole story — a denial mostly means the damage wasn't presented the way adjusters need to see it. When I inspect, I chalk every test square, photograph every soft-metal hit, and hand the adjuster a report they can approve without sticking their neck out. Different evidence, different outcome."

### The Psychology
Attribution shift: moves the cause of denial from "the carrier is stingy" (unfixable) to "the documentation was thin" (fixable, and fixable specifically by you). Concrete process details (chalk, test squares, soft metals) function as credibility markers.

## OBJECTION: "Won't they just drop me if I file?"
**Tags:** dropped, cancelled, non-renewal, afraid, policy
**Category:** Insurance Skepticism

### The Reframe
Address the fear with the actual rule, then flip it: the risky move is an unrepaired roof, not a filed claim.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Oklahoma law doesn't let a carrier non-renew you over a single act-of-God claim — one hail claim is normal business to them. You know what actually gets policies non-renewed? A roof with old, unrepaired storm damage at renewal inspection time. Filing and fixing it is how you stay insurable."

### The Psychology
Directly neutralizes the feared outcome with a rule-based fact, then re-assigns the fear to the do-nothing path (fear appeal aimed at inaction). "Normal business to them" normalizes the claim and shrinks its perceived audacity.
