# Never Split the Difference — Field Notes

Chris Voss's FBI negotiation toolkit, translated to the doorstep.

## Tactical empathy

Understand and VOICE their position before advancing yours. The homeowner
who feels heard stops defending.

## Mirroring

Repeat their last 1–3 words with an upward tone, then wait.
- Them: "We've just been really burned by contractors before."
- You: "Burned before?" → they elaborate → the real objection surfaces.

## Labeling

Name the emotion; never argue with it.
- "It seems like you've had a bad experience with storm companies."
- "It sounds like the timing feels overwhelming."
A correct label defuses; even a wrong one gets corrected with information.

## The accusation audit

Say the worst they're thinking before they do:
"You're probably thinking I'm another storm chaser who'll disappear after
the check clears." Naming it strips its power — then contrast with proof
(local address, license, references).

## Calibrated questions (How/What, never Why)

- "What would need to be true for this to feel safe?"
- "How would you like to handle the inspection findings?"
- "What's the biggest concern about filing?"
"Why" sounds like an accusation. How/What makes them solve your problem.

## "No" is not failure

"No" makes people feel safe and in control. Questions that invite it work:
- "Is it a ridiculous idea to have the roof looked at while I'm already
  on the street?"
- "Have you given up on getting the storm damage handled?"

## "That's right" > "You're right"

Summarize their situation so accurately they say "that's right" — that's
buy-in. ("You're right" means "please stop talking.")

## Late-night FM DJ voice

Downward inflection, calm, slow — especially when THEY get heated
(adjuster disputes, spouse disagreements). Tone is contagious.

## The 7-38-55 rule

Words 7%, tone 38%, body language 55%. Rehearse delivery, not just lines.
