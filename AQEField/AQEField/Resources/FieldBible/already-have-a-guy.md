# Already Have A Guy

## OBJECTION: "We already have a roofer we use"
**Tags:** roofer, already have, loyal, our guy, contractor
**Category:** Already Have A Guy

### The Reframe
Never attack "their guy" — attack the job type. Repair roofers and insurance-claim specialists are different trades.

### Word-for-Word Response
"That's great — hold onto him, good roofers are gold. Quick question though: is he a repair roofer or an insurance-claim guy? Meeting adjusters, documenting test squares, fighting supplements — that's a different trade, and it's all I do. Get my free inspection and my documentation, and you can even hand it to your guy — you'll just know what the claim is actually worth first."

### The Psychology
Sidesteps loyalty (which you can't beat) by re-segmenting the market so "your guy" and you aren't competitors. The give-away offer ("hand it to your guy") is a costly signal of confidence that usually earns the whole job.

## OBJECTION: "My brother-in-law does roofing"
**Tags:** family, brother-in-law, relative, friend does it, cheap
**Category:** Already Have A Guy

### The Reframe
Family does labor; you do the claim. Position yourself upstream of the swing-a-hammer decision.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Even better — free labor is unbeatable, and I'm not here to compete with family. But before anybody climbs up there, you want the insurance claim scoped and approved, because whatever the carrier misses comes out of your pocket — or his. Let me handle the adjuster and maximize the scope, and who installs the shingles afterward is entirely your family's call."

### The Psychology
Removes the either/or frame that makes them defensive of family. "Comes out of your pocket — or his" quietly introduces the risk of putting an unscoped job on a relative, letting them feel the problem without you criticizing anyone.

## OBJECTION: "We got three bids already"
**Tags:** bids, quotes, shopping, comparing, estimate
**Category:** Already Have A Guy

### The Reframe
Bids are for cash jobs. On an insurance claim, the scope — not the bid — decides everything, so compete on scope.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Smart — always get bids. But can I ask: bids against what scope? On an insurance job the carrier's scope sheet decides what gets paid, and three bids on an incomplete scope are three prices for the wrong roof. I'll review your adjuster's scope for free — if items are missing, and they usually are, that's thousands back to you no matter who you hire."

### The Psychology
Reframes their diligence as incomplete rather than wrong, preserving their ego while creating doubt. The free scope review is a Trojan-horse offer: it costs them nothing, positions you as the expert auditor, and puts you last-in — the strongest position in a bid war.

## OBJECTION: "The company that did our roof ten years ago will handle it"
**Tags:** previous roofer, warranty, original installer, loyal
**Category:** Already Have A Guy

### The Reframe
Their loyalty assumes the old company still exists, still does storm work, and will show up. Test the assumption gently.

### Word-for-Word Response
"That's the right instinct — start with who you trust. Two quick things to check when you call them: are they still doing insurance restoration, and can they meet your adjuster on the roof? A lot of install crews don't touch claims. If they hesitate on either question, keep my card — the inspection and adjuster meeting are free, and your old roofer can still do the install."

### The Psychology
Plants specific diagnostic questions that their current option will likely fail, letting the homeowner discover the gap themselves (self-persuasion beats being told). You exit as the helpful backstop, not the pushy alternative — which is who they call when the assumption cracks.
