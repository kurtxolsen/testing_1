# Storm Chaser Distrust

## OBJECTION: "You're just a storm chaser — you'll be gone in six months"
**Tags:** storm chaser, out of state, fly by night, gone, distrust
**Category:** Storm Chaser Distrust

### The Reframe
Don't argue the label — out-prove it. Local proof beats verbal reassurance every time.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Fair concern — after every storm this town fills up with out-of-state tailgates, and you should be suspicious of every one of them. Here's my Oklahoma contractor registration, our office is on [local street], and I can text you three homeowners within a mile of here we roofed over a year ago — call any of them tonight. Judge us by who vouches for us, not by who knocked."

### The Psychology
Agreeing with the underlying fear ("you should be suspicious") builds instant credibility — you're on their side of the table. Verifiable local proof (registration, address, referenceable neighbors) is costly signaling a fly-by-night can't fake. Neighbors-as-references leverages social proof at its most potent range: one mile.

## OBJECTION: "I've heard about roofers taking deposits and disappearing"
**Tags:** scam, deposit, disappear, fraud, stolen money
**Category:** Storm Chaser Distrust

### The Reframe
Their fear is losing money upfront. Kill the fear structurally: align your payment schedule with their protection.

### Word-for-Word Response
"You've heard right — it happens after every storm, and it makes my job harder too. So here's how we take that risk off the table: you pay nothing out of pocket until material is delivered to your driveway, and the final payment only moves when the work passes your walkthrough. The money follows the roof, not the other way around."

### The Psychology
Structural reassurance beats verbal reassurance: changing *when* money moves removes the scam mechanism entirely rather than asking for trust. "The money follows the roof" is a portable phrase they can repeat to a skeptical spouse — give them the sentence that wins the kitchen-table debate.

## OBJECTION: "Why are you knocking doors if you're a legit company?"
**Tags:** door to door, knocking, salesman, legit, why here
**Category:** Storm Chaser Distrust

### The Reframe
Own the channel proudly. Canvassing after a storm is how legitimate restoration works, because damage is invisible from the ground.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Honest answer? Because hail damage doesn't leak for two or three years, so nobody calls a roofer — they find out when the ceiling stains, after the filing window closed. After a confirmed storm we walk the affected streets because that's when an inspection can still get you a full roof instead of a denied claim. Knocking is the early-warning system."

### The Psychology
Answers the meta-objection (distrust of the channel) with a mechanism the homeowner can verify: hail damage is latent, filing windows are real. Recasting the knock as "early-warning system" flips the salesman frame to a service frame — same behavior, opposite attribution.

## OBJECTION: "How do I know your work is any good?"
**Tags:** quality, workmanship, proof, reviews, warranty
**Category:** Storm Chaser Distrust

### The Reframe
Don't claim quality — hand them the ways to verify it and the recourse if you're wrong.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Don't take my word for it — that's what the internet is for. Scan this card: our reviews, our license, and photos of local jobs are all one tap away. And when we do your roof, you get the manufacturer's warranty on the shingles plus our workmanship warranty in writing — if I'm wrong about our quality, you have it on paper that it's my problem, not yours."

### The Psychology
Transferring verification to third parties (reviews, manufacturer certification) removes the "salesman says so" discount on every claim you make. The written warranty converts an unfalsifiable promise into an enforceable one — accountability is the trust shortcut.
