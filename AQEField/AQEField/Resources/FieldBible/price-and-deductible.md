# Price & Deductible

## OBJECTION: "I can't afford my deductible right now"
**Tags:** price, deductible, money, afford, broke
**Category:** Price & Deductible

### The Reframe
They're not saying no — they're revealing their real decision criterion. Shift from cost to timing and consequence.

### Word-for-Word Response
"I completely understand — and honestly, that's exactly why we should file now rather than later. Your deductible doesn't change whether we do this today or in six months. What does change is that unrepaired hail damage compounds — and if you wait past your carrier's filing window, that deductible becomes the full cost of a new roof. Let's at least get the inspection documented so the clock works for you instead of against you."

### The Psychology
Loss aversion (Kahneman): framing inaction as the costlier path. Deadline scarcity is legitimate here — carriers do enforce filing windows — so it's persuasion, not manipulation. The phrase "the clock works for you" converts a threat frame into an ally frame.

## OBJECTION: "How much is this going to cost me?"
**Tags:** price, cost, money, quote, estimate
**Category:** Price & Deductible

### The Reframe
They're pricing a roof; you're offering a claim. Move the conversation from retail cost to what insurance already owes them.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Great question — if the storm damage qualifies, your out-of-pocket is your deductible, period, and your insurance covers the rest. That's the whole reason I start with a free inspection instead of a sales pitch. If there's no qualifying damage, I'll tell you that too and you've lost nothing but fifteen minutes."

### The Psychology
Anchoring: replace the imagined $15–25k roof price with the deductible number before they lock onto the big figure. Offering the "no damage, no pitch" out signals honesty and lowers resistance (reactance reduction).

## OBJECTION: "Another company said they'd waive my deductible"
**Tags:** deductible, waive, competitor, illegal, discount
**Category:** Price & Deductible

### The Reframe
This is a compliance trap for the homeowner, not a discount. Position yourself as the one protecting them.

### Word-for-Word Response
"I'm glad you told me that, because you need to know: deductible waiving is insurance fraud in Oklahoma — it's the homeowner who signs the claim paperwork, so it's the homeowner taking the risk. Any contractor willing to commit fraud to win your job will cut corners you can't see from the ground. We do it by the book, and the book still gets you a full roof for the price of your deductible."

### The Psychology
Reframes the competitor's "advantage" as a liability transferred onto the homeowner. Fear appeal is legitimate because the legal exposure is real. "Cut corners you can't see" links ethics to workmanship — one judgment stands in for the other (halo effect, inverted).

## OBJECTION: "We just don't have it in the budget this year"
**Tags:** budget, money, timing, afford, next year
**Category:** Price & Deductible

### The Reframe
"Budget" assumes this is a purchase. Re-classify it as an insurance benefit they already paid for through premiums.

### Word-for-Word Response
"That's fair — but here's the thing: you've already been budgeting for this roof every month for years. It's called your premium. The storm turned that premium into a benefit you're entitled to claim, and the only budget item is the deductible. Let's find out what you're owed before we talk about what anything costs."

### The Psychology
Sunk-cost leverage flipped positive: premiums they've paid become an asset to recover, not money gone. "What you're owed" activates entitlement/fairness instincts, which are far stronger motivators than savings.
