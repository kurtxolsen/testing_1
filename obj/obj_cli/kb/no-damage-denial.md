# No Damage Denial

## OBJECTION: "Our roof is fine — it's not leaking"
**Tags:** no damage, fine, not leaking, no problem, roof is good
**Category:** No Damage Denial

### The Reframe
Leaks are the last symptom, not the first. Move their test for damage from "water in the house" to "granules and bruising."

### Word-for-Word Response
"That's the tricky part about hail — a bruised shingle doesn't leak this year, it leaks in year three, long after your claim window closed. The damage that totals a roof is granule loss you can only see up close. Your gutters and downspouts will tell us in five minutes: if the soft metals took hits, the shingles did too."

### The Psychology
Replaces their damage heuristic (leak = damage) with the technically correct one (impact = damage, leak = late symptom) — you're educating, not contradicting. The gutter check is a five-minute, ground-level, verifiable test: tiny commitment, immediate evidence.

## OBJECTION: "The storm wasn't that bad here"
**Tags:** storm, not bad, small hail, missed us, weather
**Category:** No Damage Denial

### The Reframe
Their evidence is what they saw from the window. Counter with data and the neighborhood pattern, not opinion.

### Word-for-Word Response
"From the ground it never looks bad — but the weather service logged inch-and-a-quarter hail on this square mile, and I've marked damage on four roofs on this street this week. Hail falls in swaths, so your neighbor can get hammered while you get grazed — which is exactly why a free fifteen-minute look beats guessing."

### The Psychology
Third-party data (NOAA reports) plus local social proof ("four roofs on this street") outweighs a memory of the storm. "Swaths" gives them a face-saving mechanism to be wrong: they're not mistaken about the storm, hail is just patchy.

## OBJECTION: "We just had the roof replaced two years ago"
**Tags:** new roof, replaced, recent, two years, brand new
**Category:** No Damage Denial

### The Reframe
New roofs get hailed on too — and a damaged new roof is a bigger stake, not a smaller one.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Even more reason to check it — hail doesn't card your roof for its age, and a two-year-old roof with bruising is a fifteen-year roof that fails in eight. Worse, undocumented storm damage can void your manufacturer warranty, because they'll blame the hail instead of their shingle. Fifteen minutes protects a brand-new investment; that's the best inspection math there is."

### The Psychology
Judo move: their reason for complacency (new roof) becomes the reason for urgency (bigger unprotected investment). The warranty-voiding point introduces a risk they've never considered, and protecting a recent purchase triggers loss aversion harder than improving an old one ever could.

## OBJECTION: "Someone already looked at it and said it's fine"
**Tags:** already inspected, second opinion, another roofer, said fine
**Category:** No Damage Denial

### The Reframe
An inspection is only as good as its documentation. Ask what evidence they were shown — usually the answer is none.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Good — I'm glad somebody's looked. Quick question: did they leave you photos, or chalk any test squares, or check the soft metals — or was it a walk-around and a thumbs up? A real inspection produces evidence you can hold. I'll put my findings in writing with photos either way — if I agree it's clean, you've got a documented baseline for the next storm, free."

### The Psychology
Never attack the other inspector — raise the standard of proof until their inspection falls short of it on its own. The "clean baseline" framing means you win even by finding nothing, which makes the offer feel like service rather than a hunt for a sale.
