# Adjuster Pushback

## OBJECTION: "I'm only seeing seven hits per square — that's below repair threshold"
**Tags:** adjuster, hits per square, threshold, repair, count
**Category:** Adjuster Pushback

### The Reframe
Don't argue the count — expand the evidence. More test squares, more slopes, plus collateral damage that corroborates impact density.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Fair count on that square — let's chalk two more on this slope, because hail density isn't uniform and one square isn't a sample. And before we settle on repair, look at the soft metals: the downspouts and AC fins took hits at a density that doesn't match a seven-per-square storm. If the collateral says heavy impact, the shingle count deserves a second sample."

### The Psychology
Conceding the first data point ("fair count") buys standing to demand a bigger sample — you're arguing methodology, not honesty. Adjusters can defend a scope change to their desk reviewer if the file shows objective corroboration; you're literally handing them the paper trail their approval needs.

## OBJECTION: "That's mechanical damage, not hail"
**Tags:** adjuster, mechanical damage, wear, denial, cause
**Category:** Adjuster Pushback

### The Reframe
Causation disputes are won with pattern evidence: randomness, directionality, and matching collateral — not louder opinions.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Let's test that against the pattern — mechanical damage tracks foot traffic and tool paths, but these bruises are randomly distributed across the whole slope, including areas nobody walks. They're also concentrated on the storm-facing exposure, and they match the dents on the gutters, which nobody hit with a hammer. Random distribution, directional bias, matching collateral — that's a hail signature, and I'd like it noted in the file."

### The Psychology
You're providing the three-factor argument (randomness, direction, collateral) the adjuster's own training uses, making agreement feel like applying their standards rather than losing to a salesman. "Noted in the file" signals you'll pursue a reinspection if needed — raising the cost of a soft denial without a single confrontational word.

## OBJECTION: "We'll pay for repairs, not replacement"
**Tags:** adjuster, repair only, partial, replace, brittle
**Category:** Adjuster Pushback

### The Reframe
Shift the question from "how many shingles are damaged" to "can this roof actually be repaired without making it worse."

### Word-for-Word Response
"Then let's do a repairability test together, because a repair scope assumes these shingles can survive one. Watch what happens when I lift this tab — the mat's brittle and the seal strip tears out granules, so every 'repair' creates two new damaged shingles. The manufacturer's own guidelines call this condition non-repairable, and I'll get you that bulletin for the file."

### The Psychology
A live demonstration beats any verbal argument — the shingle cracking in the adjuster's sight line is evidence they can't unsee. Citing the manufacturer's repairability guidelines gives them a third-party authority to lean on, converting "the contractor wants more" into "the manufacturer requires it."

## OBJECTION: "The carrier won't pay for code items like drip edge"
**Tags:** adjuster, code items, drip edge, ordinance, supplement
**Category:** Adjuster Pushback

### The Reframe
Code compliance isn't a wish-list item — it's an ordinance-and-law coverage question. Ask about the endorsement, then document.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Understood — can we confirm whether the policy carries ordinance-and-law coverage before we strike it? Drip edge is IRC code here, the city enforces it at permit, and the roof can't pass final inspection without it. If it's code-required to complete the repair, it belongs in the scope — and I'll send the code citation with photos so it's an easy supplement approval."

### The Psychology
Framing it as a coverage-verification question rather than a demand keeps the adjuster in problem-solving mode instead of gatekeeping mode. Referencing permit enforcement makes the cost unavoidable-by-law, and pre-packaging the citation lowers the adjuster's effort to approve — effort, not generosity, is usually the real barrier.
