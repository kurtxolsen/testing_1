# Bad Timing

## OBJECTION: "This isn't a good time — we're in the middle of dinner"
**Tags:** dinner, busy, bad time, interrupting, now
**Category:** Bad Timing

### The Reframe
Respect the moment instantly and trade it for a scheduled one. The win here is the appointment, not the conversation.

### Word-for-Word Response
"My apologies — go eat, I'll be quick. I'm inspecting storm damage on this street all week; your neighbors at the corner just had theirs done. I'll come back tomorrow — is right after work better, or Saturday morning?"

### The Psychology
Immediate deference earns more goodwill than any pitch could — you become "the polite one" in a category famous for pushiness. The alternative-choice close (tomorrow vs. Saturday) converts an exit into an appointment in a single breath, and the neighbor mention plants social proof to marinate over dinner.

## OBJECTION: "We're about to leave / heading out the door"
**Tags:** leaving, no time, rushed, on our way out, quick
**Category:** Bad Timing

### The Reframe
Perfect situation — the roof doesn't need them home. Decouple the inspection from their presence.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Then I'll be fast — here's the good news: you don't even need to be here. Give me the okay and I'll inspect while you're out, leave a written summary with photos at the door, and there's zero obligation either way. You come home to answers instead of a follow-up knock."

### The Psychology
Removes 100% of the time cost, which was the entire objection — hard to refuse a request that costs nothing. "Answers instead of a follow-up knock" also promises relief from future interruptions, subtly acknowledging the annoyance and monetizing it.

## OBJECTION: "Come back after the holidays"
**Tags:** holidays, later, season, busy time, postpone
**Category:** Bad Timing

### The Reframe
Their calendar concern is real, but the claim clock doesn't pause for holidays. Split the inspection (now) from the project (later).

### Word-for-Word Response
"Happy to work around the holidays — the build can absolutely wait until January. But the claim can't always wait, because carriers measure the filing window from the storm date, not from when it's convenient. Let's get the inspection and paperwork done this week — twenty minutes — and then nothing else touches your calendar until the new year."

### The Psychology
Splitting the ask (inspect now, build later) lets them keep their holiday boundary while you keep the deal alive — both sides "win" the negotiation. The filing-window deadline supplies legitimate urgency that a callback date never survives.

## OBJECTION: "Just leave a card and I'll call you"
**Tags:** leave a card, call you, brush off, later, maybe
**Category:** Bad Timing

### The Reframe
Cards go in drawers; commitments go on calendars. Accept the card request and attach one concrete anchor to it.

### Word-for-Word Response
"Absolutely — card's yours. And look, I know how cards work: it goes in the junk drawer and life happens, no offense taken. So let me earn the callback instead — I'm doing inspections on this street Thursday anyway; if I knock at 5:30 and you've got fifteen minutes, great, and if not, wave me off and I'm gone."

### The Psychology
Naming the junk-drawer reality out loud is disarming honesty — it says you're not fooled, without accusing them of lying. The Thursday knock converts a dead-end into a scheduled second touch with a pre-authorized, zero-guilt exit, which paradoxically makes people more likely to say yes when it comes.
